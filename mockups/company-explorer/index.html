Which big Swedish companies are doing enough about the climate?

Meeting the Paris Agreement means cutting emissions roughly in half this decade. Here is how Sweden’s largest listed companies are doing.

How do we decide if a company is on track?

We give every company a carbon budget — the most it can still emit if the world is to stay within 1.5 °C of warming — and check whether it is cutting fast enough to stay inside it.

Fast enough is on track. Too slow, or not cutting at all, is off track. Companies that haven’t reported enough years yet are shown as not enough data rather than hidden.

Emissions means everything a company reports — its own factories and vehicles, the energy it buys, and the emissions from its suppliers and its products. That last part is usually the biggest, which is why a bank or a clothing brand can have a far bigger footprint than you would expect. We measure it in CO₂e, which adds up all the greenhouse gases as if they were carbon dioxide.

Change so far compares the latest year with the first year the company reported. A minus number means emissions have come down.
